# Methods, sources, licences and what was measured

Last checked: 2026-10-26.

## The three methods

| Method | Type | Paper / source | Licence | Languages | Score direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `binoculars` | zero-shot, two LLMs | Hans et al., *Spotting LLMs with Binoculars*, ICML 2024, [arXiv:2401.12070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070); code [ahans30/Binoculars](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars) | code BSD-3-Clause; models Apache-2.0 (Qwen3) | any language the pair models | **lower = more AI-like** |
| `fastdetect` | zero-shot, one LLM | Bao et al., *Fast-DetectGPT*, ICLR 2024, [arXiv:2310.05130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05130); code [baoguangsheng/fast-detect-gpt](https://github.com/baoguangsheng/fast-detect-gpt) | MIT; models Apache-2.0 | any language the model covers | higher = more AI-like |
| `desklib` | supervised classifier | [desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01](https://huggingface.co/desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01), DeBERTa-v3-large fine-tuned on RAID | MIT | **English only** | probability 0–1, higher = AI |

### Binoculars

Score = log-perplexity of the text under the *performer* model divided by the
cross-perplexity between the *observer* and the *performer* (the expected log-loss of the
performer's next-token distribution under the observer's). Human text is "surprising" to
both models in a way the models do not expect of each other; machine text is not. The
reference implementation uses `tiiuae/falcon-7b` (observer) and `tiiuae/falcon-7b-instruct`
(performer) and two thresholds tuned on English: `0.9015` (F1-optimal) and `0.8536`
(0.01 % FPR). The paper reports >90 % detection of ChatGPT text at 0.01 % false positives and
says the method is "more proficient in detecting English language text compared to other
languages". `ai_models.py` reproduces the reference computation (performer log-ppl over
cross-ppl, float32 softmax, truncation to `--max-tokens`).

Model pairs here (observer = base, performer = post-trained), all Apache-2.0, all ungated;
Qwen3 lists Ukrainian among its 119 languages:

| `--size` | observer | performer | fp16 size |
|---|---|---|---|
| small | Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B-Base | Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B | ~2.4 GB |
| medium (default) | Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Base | Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B | ~7 GB |
| large | Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base | Qwen/Qwen3-4B | ~16 GB |
| falcon | tiiuae/falcon-7b | tiiuae/falcon-7b-instruct | ~28 GB (the paper's pair; English) |

### Fast-DetectGPT (analytic)

Conditional probability curvature with the same model as sampling and scoring model:
`d = (log p(x) − Σ E[log p]) / sqrt(Σ Var[log p])`, token sums over the text. The paper reports
AUROC 0.93 on ChatGPT/GPT-4 text vs 0.72 for DetectGPT, 340× faster. No universal threshold;
the authors evaluate by AUROC, so `calibrate.py` is required for a verdict.

### Desklib

A DeBERTa-v3-large encoder, mean pooling, one linear logit, trained on RAID (Dugan et al., ACL
2024). Led the RAID leaderboard at submission. English only, 768-token window. The model
card's loader class breaks on transformers 5.x; `ai_models.py` loads the safetensors
checkpoint by hand into the same architecture (keys `model.*` + `classifier.*`).

## What was measured on this machine (M2 Max, 32 GB; 2026-10-26)

Small samples; treat as sanity checks, not benchmarks. "Claude-written" = paragraphs written
by Claude Fable 5.1 on request, academic/encyclopedic register, 150–250 words each.
"Recent human" = a 2023 KPI regulation PDF and 2026 news articles (not in any model's
training data). "Wikipedia" = Ukrainian/English Wikipedia extracts.

| Human set | AI set | Method / pair | AUROC | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recent human, uk (4 chunks) | text generated by Qwen3-0.6B itself (7) | binoculars small | **1.00** | implementation correct: self-generated text scores 0.52–0.72, human 0.98–1.04 |
| recent human, uk (4) | Claude-written, uk (8) | binoculars small | 0.38 | **no separation**: Claude text 0.90–1.13 overlaps human |
| recent human, uk (4) | Claude-written, uk (8) | binoculars large (4B) | 0.56 | no separation either |
| Wikipedia uk (37) | Claude-written, uk (8) | binoculars small / medium | 0.40 / 0.40 | inverted: memorised Wikipedia looks *more* machine-like than Claude |
| Wikipedia uk (37) | Claude-written, uk (8) | fastdetect small / medium | 0.51 / 0.55 | chance |
| Wikipedia en (70) | Claude-written, en (4) | binoculars small | 0.22 | inverted, same memorisation effect |
| Wikipedia en (70) | Claude-written, en (4) | desklib | 0.91 | Claude text 0.92–1.00; but 19/70 human chunks > 0.5 and 14/70 > 0.9 |
| recent human (1) | Qwen3-0.6B-generated en (4) | desklib | 1.00 | generated text 1.00 |

Conclusions that go into every report:

1. The zero-shot detectors work as published on text sampled from small open models and do
   **not** detect 2026 frontier-model prose (Claude here; expect the same for GPT/Gemini) in
   Ukrainian or English with pairs up to 4B. The published Binoculars numbers were obtained
   with 7B models on 2023-era ChatGPT text.
2. The supervised English classifier (Desklib) does catch frontier-model English text, at the
   price of flagging a fifth of formal human encyclopedic text with probability > 0.9. It can
   rank passages; it cannot clear or accuse.
3. Memorised or template-like human text (Wikipedia, standard definitions, legal wording)
   scores as "machine" under perplexity methods. This is the main false-positive mechanism
   in a dissertation (methods sections, definitions, regulatory quotes).
4. Nothing here is validated on Ukrainian academic prose by anyone; the one 2025 study found
   (*Software detection of Ukrainian-language texts generated by AI*, ResearchGate
   395029123) reports "significant challenges" for all tested tools on Ukrainian.
